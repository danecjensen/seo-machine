The home office deduction lets freelancers deduct part of their rent or mortgage interest, utilities and insurance when part of the home is used only for work. It's one of the most valuable deductions for people who work from home, and one of the most misunderstood.

## Who qualifies

The space must be used regularly and exclusively for business. A spare bedroom that is only an office qualifies. A kitchen table you also eat at doesn't. It must also be your principal place of business, which it usually is if you do your admin and most of your work there.

## The simplified method

Multiply the square footage of your office, up to 300 square feet, by $5. A 150 square foot office gives a $750 deduction. There's no depreciation to track and very little record keeping, which makes this the right choice for most renters with small offices.

## The regular method

Work out the percentage of your home the office takes up, then deduct that share of rent or mortgage interest, utilities, insurance, repairs and depreciation. A 200 square foot office in a 1,600 square foot home is 12.5%. On $24,000 of rent and $3,600 of utilities, that's $3,450. The regular method usually wins in high-rent cities.

## Records to keep

Keep a floor plan or measurements, a photo of the space, and a year of rent, utility and insurance statements. If you own the home, keep your closing documents too, because depreciation affects your tax when you sell.

## Putting it to work

The deduction lowers your profit, which lowers both income tax and self-employment tax. Add it to your estimate when you plan [quarterly estimated taxes](/blog/quarterly-estimated-taxes/) so you don't overpay during the year.
