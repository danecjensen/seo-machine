Quarterly estimated taxes are how freelancers pay income and self-employment tax during the year, since no employer is withholding it for you. If you expect to owe at least $1,000 when you file, the IRS expects four payments spread across the year.

## Who needs to pay

You need to make estimated payments if you're self-employed and expect to owe $1,000 or more in federal tax after subtracting any withholding and credits. That includes freelancers, independent contractors and single-member LLC owners.

## The due dates

Payments are due in mid-April, mid-June, mid-September and mid-January of the following year. When a date falls on a weekend or holiday, it moves to the next business day. Notice the uneven spacing: the second payment comes only two months after the first.

## How to calculate a payment

Start with your expected profit for the year: income minus business expenses. Self-employment tax is about 15.3% of roughly 92% of that profit. Add your income tax using your expected bracket, then divide by four. Ledgerly does this math continuously using your real numbers, which matters when income is uneven.

## Avoiding penalties with the safe harbor

You won't owe an underpayment penalty if your payments cover 100% of last year's total tax (110% if your income was over $150,000), or 90% of this year's. Paying last year's tax in four equal parts is the simplest way to stay safe while your income is unpredictable.

## A simple routine

Each time a client pays you, move 25 to 30 percent to a separate savings account. Before each due date, pay from that account. Track it all in a [freelancer expense tracker](/features/) so your deductions lower each payment.
